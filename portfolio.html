Chapter Two · The Question
An Alert Says “Suspicious”.
It Doesn't Say Why.

A fraud analyst may have only moments to decide. A score alone cannot tell them whether to trust it. That gap became my MSc research. Switch between the two views to see the difference an explanation makes.

Blocked Mobile Banking · NGN 495,886 Fraud probability 0.89

No further information. The analyst has to investigate the account to find out why.

The amount is significantly above this customer's typical range, and spending over the last 24 hours is high.

  • Transaction amount+0.137
  • 24-hour spend+0.104
  • Amount vs usual+0.095

It would not be flagged if the amount were below about NGN 170,000.

A test-set example from my dissertation, using synthetic data.

MSc Dissertation · Wrexham University · 2026

Explainable AI with Human-in-the-Loop Feedback for Real-Time Fraud Detection in Nigerian Inter-Bank Payment Systems

The problem: fraud models can flag a transaction without telling the analyst why, and they do not learn from the analyst's decision.

What I did:

  • Prepared 1,000,000 synthetic transactions calibrated to NIBSS statistics, with only 3,000 fraud cases, and tested on later months than the models trained on.
  • Built and compared detection models using Isolation Forest, Random Forest, XGBoost and ADASYN oversampling.
  • Added SHAP explanations in plain language, with "what would need to change" counterfactuals for each transaction.
  • Designed a feedback loop that sends borderline cases to analysts and retrains the model on their answers, and evaluated it in simulation.

Results: the best model (XGBoost) caught 66% of fraud cases in the test period with no false positives. Explanations were produced in about 340 ms at the 95th percentile. In the simulated feedback loop, the share of fraud blocked automatically rose from 44.5% to 70.4%.

Tools: Python · XGBoost · Random Forest · Isolation Forest · SHAP
